What gets demoted

Disease heatmaps, regulator layers, and regional outbreak signals are still useful, but they should sit as the expansion path once the practice workflow is embedded.

Do not pitch “platform for farms, vets, and regulators” on page one.
Do not lead with surveillance if the daily dispensing job is still messy.
Do not make the first impression “nice map”. Make it “finally, control”.
Practice workflow
From order request to inspection-ready record
The demo still uses the same underlying farm data, but the story is now about the operating loop TAH actually cares about. This is the wedge worth buying.
1
Farm submits monthly medicine request
Orders come in through a controlled channel instead of scattered calls and messages. The practice sees who asked for what, when, and why.
2
Practice approves and dispenses against history
Before product leaves the shelf, the team can check prior use, assigned vet, and whether the request makes operational sense.
3
Withdrawal periods stay live until resolved
The important thing is not logging the treatment. It is knowing what is still unsafe to ship, sell, or forget.
4
Reports are generated from the workflow, not rebuilt after it
Registers, audit trails, and inspection responses fall out of daily use. No end-of-month cleanup panic.
